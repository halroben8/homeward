HOMEWARD
Game Design Document
Version 1.0

========================================
1. OVERVIEW
========================================

Title: Homeward
Genre: Cute exploration / light stealth adventure
Platform: PC
Tone: Lighthearted, charming, low-stakes. Getting “taken” is an annoying detour, never scary or punishing.

Premise:
You are a cute orange tabby cat whose collar shows your home address at the start of the game. You have wandered away and must navigate a city to find your way back. The only real “danger” is well-meaning people who will try to take you home with them if you get too close or linger too long.

========================================
2. CORE LOOP
========================================

1. At the start, the camera shows a close-up of the cat’s collar with the home address clearly displayed (example: “14 Maple Street, Apartment 3B”).
2. The player is placed somewhere in the city and must navigate streets, alleys, parks, and rooftops to find that address.
3. People on the streets may notice the cat and try to pick it up.
4. If a person successfully takes the cat, the player is brought to that person’s home and must complete a short skill-based escape puzzle.
5. After escaping, that specific person will actively look for the cat for exactly 60 seconds.
6. Reach the correct address → win (heartwarming homecoming with the real owner).

========================================
3. PROTAGONIST
========================================

- Cute cartoon-style orange tabby cat
- Big expressive green eyes
- Fluffy striped fur, white muzzle, chest, and paws
- Black collar with a small round metal address tag
- Movement feels distinctly cat-like: low to the ground, climbing, squeezing under objects, short bursts of speed
- Can re-check the collar address at any time (requires stopping for a moment)

========================================
4. PEOPLE (NPCS) & THE “TAKE HOME” SYSTEM
========================================

People are the only threat. They are all well-meaning and cute in presentation.

Types of NPCs:
- Cat-loving young woman
- Excited kids
- Friendly dog walkers (dog may bark and cause the cat to startle)
- Cheerful shopkeepers
- Ordinary pedestrians (less aggressive)

How they take the cat:
- Getting too close for too long
- Lingering in their path
- Being cornered
- Optional risk/reward actions (e.g. meowing)

When taken:
- Short cute animation of the person carrying the cat to their home
- Player arrives inside and must solve a short escape puzzle

========================================
5. ESCAPE PUZZLES
========================================

Short (15–45 seconds), tactile, skill-based mini-puzzles using the cat’s normal movement abilities. Always succeed eventually; failure just restarts the sequence.

Example escape types:
- Sliding bolt / lever latch (jump onto furniture and paw it open)
- Window catch (climb and time a claw/flip)
- Pet door / flap (push and slip through)
- Carrier or temporary collar clip (wriggle free)
- Light multi-step (knock something down to create a boost or reach)

Homes are small, cozy, and varied (apartment, house porch, shop back room, etc.). Soft lighting, personal objects, gentle music.

========================================
6. 60-SECOND PURSUIT
========================================

After a successful escape:
- The person who took the cat gets a clear but cute visual indicator (soft heart or “!”) and may call out in a friendly voice.
- For exactly 60 seconds they will actively walk toward the cat if they have line of sight and try to pick it up again.
- The player can hide, climb, sprint, or use normal movement to avoid them.
- After 60 seconds the indicator disappears and the person returns to normal pedestrian behavior.
- Soft limit of simultaneous pursuers (recommended max 2) so it never feels overwhelming.

========================================
7. NAVIGATION & WORLD
========================================

- City neighborhoods with readable landmarks (parks, shops, street signs, bus stops, etc.)
- Shortcuts: fences, rooftops, alleys, under cars, through parks
- Safe spots where people are less likely to notice the cat (under cars, on high ledges, empty lots)
- Daytime or golden-hour lighting
- Street signs and environmental clues help the player remember or rediscover the address

========================================
8. CONTROLS & ABILITIES
========================================

- Low, cat-eye camera
- Walk / sprint
- Jump / climb onto objects and ledges
- Squeeze under low obstacles
- Paw / claw interactions for puzzles
- Check collar (stop and look down)
- Optional meow (can attract or distract)

========================================
9. TONE & PRESENTATION
========================================

- Everything stays cute and light
- People never look angry — just determined and a little disappointed when the cat escapes
- Soft, playful music even during escapes and short pursuits
- Character designs use big eyes, soft colors, and friendly proportions (matching the established cat and NPC concept art)

========================================
10. WIN CONDITION
========================================

Reach the correct address shown on the collar. Short, warm homecoming scene with the real owner.

========================================
11. FUTURE IDEAS (NOT YET LOCKED)
========================================

- Multiple cat variants with slight ability differences
- Collectible lost tags or photos of the real owner
- Soft checkpoints or “last safe spot” restarts
- Different city districts with increasing pedestrian density

END OF DOCUMENT
